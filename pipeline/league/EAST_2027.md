# Eastern heat clock, 2027: the rule, written before the season (2026-10-25, revised 2026-10-26 after review)

**Why.** Hatch will grow past the Midwest. On past seasons a local heat clock beat each eastern place's calendar by
about 1.6 days ([tryouts/REGIONAL_RESULT.md](tryouts/REGIONAL_RESULT.md)), but that idea was found after looking at
the data. 2027 is its honest test: nobody has seen 2027's moths yet.

**What runs.** From 2 March to 7 October 2027, every night after the map's own files are written (`east.py`, run by
`pipeline/live/scorecard.py` in its own process, killed after 15 minutes): the clock frozen in `east_static.json`
forecasts the onset (10% of first-brood sightings) and peak (50%) for each of its 28 eastern places (2° latitude ×
6° longitude cells outside the seven Midwest states, each with at least one 2015-2025 season of 20+ first-brood
records from 1 March on), on real PRISM weather to date (read as the map's forecast reads it), the 7-day forecast,
then each 2015-2025 year as an ending. Only those dates are kept, sealed per night in `live/archive_east/2027/`.
Nothing appears on the map.

**How it is graded.** Weekly, by the live scorecard's own `score_band`, unchanged: a place is scored once its first
brood is over and its records are 14 days old, with at least 20 research-grade first-brood records (P1's filters,
the place's frozen brood cut-off, nothing before 1 March), against the newest sealed forecast issued at least 7 days
before each real date, and frozen the week it is first scored. Only envelopes sealed by the frozen file count. Its
rival is the place's calendar (its mean 2015-2025 onset and peak, frozen in `east_static.json`). Each week's records
pull is kept with its sha256 (`live/records/2027/east/`). A place whose real onset comes before the first forecast
can be 7 days old (early March, the far South) is not scored, for either side.

**The verdict** (week of Monday 27 September 2027; later records never count):
- Headline: record-weighted mean miss (mean of onset and peak miss) over scored places, D = clock − calendar.
- Fewer than **10** scored places or **1,000** records: no verdict.
- D ≤ **−1.0** day and the clock has the strictly lower miss in **at least half** of the scored places (equal misses
  at the scorecard's 0.1-day rounding count as not won): the clock wins and is what an eastern layer uses when one
  is built (from 2028).
- Otherwise the calendar stays. That means the clock did not prove itself, not that it is worse. A clock that loses
  is not retuned and retried as if new.
- Reported beside the verdict, never deciding it: the unweighted place mean D and a 95% interval of D resampling
  places. One season's places share one year's weather, so even a pass is one good year, not proof.
- Attempts: if 2027 gives no verdict, the same frozen file is tested once more in 2028 (move `SEASONS` and `VERDICT`
  in `east.py` a year on, before 1 March 2028, the only change allowed), as a fresh season, not pooled with 2027.
  There is no third attempt.

**Frozen.** `east_static.json` sha256 prefix: `19febf953230` (28 places, 198 hexes; built by `build_east.py`; PRISM
covers the US only, so Canadian places and hexes are left out). `test_east.py` holds the file and the rule's numbers
(1.0, 10, 1,000, 27 Sep 2027) to this text. Changing either after 1 March 2027 voids the test for 2027. The 2016,
2020 and 2024 endings are indexed by day of year from 29 Feb, as P3's.

**Rehearsal on 2026** (`rehearse_east.py`, a season the frozen file never saw; sealed every night from 3 March on
PRISM to two days before, with no 7-day forecast; graded as 2027 will be): 24 places scored, 4,088 records. Clock
6.24 d, calendar 7.89 d, D = −1.65, but the clock had the lower miss in only 11 of 24 places, so under this rule
**the calendar would have stayed** (it needed 12). The unweighted place mean D is −1.16 and the interval over places
is −4.13 to +0.72: the record-weighted gain leans on a few places where 2026 ran far from normal (33-35/-88 1.6 vs
11.3, 39-41/-82 6.5 vs 14.5, 37-39/-88 10.7 vs 24.1). Where 2026 was near normal the calendar won, by up to 3.8 days
(the Northeast: 43-45/-76 7.0 vs 3.2, 43-45/-82 5.7 vs 2.0). The four 31-33 °N places were not scored (onset before
the first usable forecast). Caveats: PRISM and record grades are read as they stand now, not as they stood each
night. The rule's thresholds were first written before this run, and the review changes since (the 10-place and
1,000-record floors, the side statistics, the attempt limit) only make a pass harder; git shows one commit, so the
order is stated, not proven. Rows: `EAST_REHEARSAL_2026.json`.

**What this does not test.** Brand-new places with no past season (there the shared warmth curve did best on past
data, 7.1 d) and the Midwest map, whose 2027 test is [PREREG_2027.md](../fit/PREREG_2027.md), untouched.
