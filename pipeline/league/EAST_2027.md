# Eastern heat clock, 2027: the rule, written before the season (2026-10-25)

**Why.** Hatch will grow past the Midwest. On past seasons a local heat clock beat each eastern place's calendar by
about 1.5 days ([tryouts/REGIONAL_RESULT.md](tryouts/REGIONAL_RESULT.md)), but that idea was found after looking at
the data. 2027 is its honest test: nobody has seen 2027's moths yet.

**What runs.** From 2 March to 7 October 2027, every night after the map's own forecast (`east.py`, called by
`pipeline/live/scorecard.py`): the clock frozen in `east_static.json` forecasts the onset (10% of first-brood
sightings) and peak (50%) for each of its eastern places (2° latitude × 6° longitude cells outside the seven
Midwest states, each with at least one 2015-2025 season of 20+ first-brood records), on real PRISM weather to date,
the 7-day forecast, then each 2015-2025 year as an ending. Only those dates are kept, sealed per night in
`live/archive_east/2027/`. Nothing appears on the map.

**How it is graded.** Weekly, by the live scorecard's own `score_band`, unchanged: a place is scored once its first
brood is over and its records are 14 days old, with at least 20 research-grade first-brood records (P1's filters,
the place's frozen brood cut-off), against the newest sealed forecast issued at least 7 days before each real date,
and frozen the week it is first scored. Its rival is the place's calendar (the mean 2015-2025 onset and peak, frozen
in `east_static.json`). Each week's records pull is kept with its sha256 (`live/records/2027/east/`).

**The verdict** (week of Monday 27 September 2027; later records never count):
- Headline: record-weighted mean miss (mean of onset and peak miss) over scored places, D = clock − calendar.
- Fewer than **5** scored places or **300** records: no verdict; the test repeats in 2028 with the same frozen file.
- D ≤ **−1.0** day and the clock has the lower miss in **at least half** of the scored places: the clock wins and
  is what an eastern layer uses when one is built (from 2028).
- Otherwise the calendar stays. A clock that loses is not retuned and retried as if new.

**Frozen.** `east_static.json` sha256 prefix: `d09be1f01c8e` (28 places, 198 hexes; built by `build_east.py`; PRISM covers
the US only, so Canadian places and hexes are left out). `test_east.py` holds the file and the rule's numbers (1.0,
5, 300, 27 Sep 2027) to this text. Changing either after 1 March 2027 voids the test for 2027.

**Rehearsal on 2026** (`rehearse_east.py`, a season the frozen file never saw; sealed every night from 3 March on
PRISM to two days before, with no 7-day forecast; graded as 2027 will be): 24 places scored, 4,088 records. Clock
6.37 d, calendar 7.74 d, D = −1.38, but the clock had the lower miss in only 11 of 24 places, so under this rule
**the calendar would have stayed** (it needed 12). It won big where 2026 ran far from normal (the Deep South and the
Ohio valley: 33-35/-88 2.0 vs 11.1, 39-41/-82 6.5 vs 14.4) and lost narrowly where it was near normal (the
Northeast, where the calendar missed by 1-3 days). The rule above was written before this run and is not changed by
it. Rows: `EAST_REHEARSAL_2026.json`.

**What this does not test.** Brand-new places with no past season (there the shared warmth curve did best on past
data, 7.1 d) and the Midwest map, whose 2027 test is [PREREG_2027.md](../fit/PREREG_2027.md), untouched.
